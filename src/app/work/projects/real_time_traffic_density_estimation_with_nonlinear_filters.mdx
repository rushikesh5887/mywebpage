## Project Summary

This was a collaborative project on real-time traffic density estimation for heterogeneous Indian urban traffic, where direct field measurement of density is difficult and noisy. The study evaluated model-based recursive estimation approaches for inferring density from more easily measurable traffic variables.

The project compared `EKF`, `Particle Filter`, and `Unscented Kalman Filter` approaches under nonlinear traffic conditions and validated the estimates against manually extracted field density values. It also benchmarked the filters against a base model derived from fundamental traffic flow relations.

## Business and Research Impact

Traffic density is a critical congestion indicator, but it is difficult to measure directly in real-world urban traffic, especially under heterogeneous and non-lane-disciplined conditions. This project showed how nonlinear recursive estimation methods can improve density inference when field measurements are noisy, incomplete, or impractical to obtain continuously.

This project adds evidence of mathematical modeling depth, state-space thinking, and the ability to work on estimation problems where model design, uncertainty handling, and validation all matter.

## Skills and Technologies

- **State-Space Modeling:** Recursive estimation, nonlinear system modeling, and traffic-state inference
- **Filtering Methods:** `Unscented Kalman Filter`, `Extended Kalman Filter`, `Particle Filter`
- **MATLAB:** Algorithm implementation, performance comparison, and validation analysis
- **Validation and Analysis:** Convergence assessment, model benchmarking, and field-data validation

## What I Built

- **UKF implementation:** Contributed the `Unscented Kalman Filter` implementation for nonlinear traffic state estimation.
- **Comparative estimation workflow:** Supported side-by-side comparison of `UKF`, `EKF`, and `Particle Filter` methods under heterogeneous traffic conditions.
- **Validation analysis:** Helped validate estimated density values against manually extracted field observations.
- **Performance and convergence study:** Contributed to interpretation of estimator stability, convergence behavior, and practical suitability.

## Challenges and Learnings

One of the main challenges was adapting recursive estimation methods to traffic conditions that are more nonlinear and less structured than standard freeway settings. That made both the estimator behavior and the validation process more demanding.

This work highlighted how valuable model-based estimation can be when direct sensing is difficult, and it strengthened my experience with nonlinear filtering, comparative algorithm analysis, and collaborative technical research.

## Outcome

The project showed that nonlinear filters, especially the `Unscented Kalman Filter`, can provide reliable traffic-density estimates in challenging urban mixed-traffic conditions. It contributed to a practical framework for real-time traffic-state estimation and demonstrates my ability to work on mathematically grounded modeling problems with real validation constraints.
